Flexible fiber microlaser for finger-joint health detection

A stress sensor made from a stretchable optical fiber. Tiny microbubble cavities in a dye-doped PMMA fiber act as lasers. When the fiber stretches, the cavities deform and the spacing between laser modes grows, so the spectrum reports the stress.

I fabricated the fibers and tuned the polymer: I compared 15k, 120k and 350k molecular-weight PMMA and chose 350k for its strength and stretch. I then characterized lasing and stress response under a laser microscope with a spectrometer, and showed how fibers along a finger could estimate joint angles for hand-mobility assessment.

Nonlinear vibrational imaging systems for chemical-specific microscopy

Two microscopes that see molecules by their vibrations. VREF pairs an infrared pump with a visible probe to turn vibrational energy into fluorescence. SRP detects the tiny heat a Raman excitation leaves behind, which makes weak Raman signals strong enough to image.

I engineer the beam paths for both systems around a Carbide Yb laser (1030, 800 and 1500 nm), with polarization optics, delay stages and PMTs. I characterized difference frequency generation for VREF's tunable mid-IR, measured SRP spectra in the C–D silent region, and run quantitative fluorescence image analysis on C. elegans to map lysosome-related organelles.
